STATISTICS is the last tab still holding F1's empty cells. §7.3 settles what goes
in it, in one sentence and four figures:

> A **dedicated section of its own**: best and worst tank, km per day, projected
> annual cost, true cost per km including purchase price — computed over

Three of those four are sums and ratios F9 already knows how to take. The fourth
is the only figure in TRITIUM that looks forward, and the only one that spends
the purchase price. Both need saying carefully, and both are said below.

## 1. WHERE F10 SITS

### 1.1 The version — and the roll

**F10 is v0.2.0**, not v0.1.10.

§9.1's versions are a **decimal roll**: the printed table runs `F10 → v0.1.9`,
`F11 → v0.2.0`, so the patch digit is a single decimal that carries into the
minor. Riding F4b's correction (`F(n) → v0.1.(n)` from F5) gives F8 → v0.1.8 and
F9 → v0.1.9, and the next one rolls. **v0.1.10 would not be a version this
project uses**, and asserting it would break the roll for every milestone after.

`issues.md` I-10 has tracked §9.1's stale table since F4b. This milestone is the
first place the staleness costs a *shape* rather than a digit, which makes it a
better moment than most for the maker's pen.

F9's lifetime totals **exclude the purchase price**, and its document says why:
folding two million lira into a page of fuel figures would make every other
number on it unreadable. §7.3 asks for a figure that includes it, and this is
where it belongs — on its own tab, beside the observation window that makes it
mean something.

## 2. SCOPE — IN

### 2.1 The decisions this milestone settles

1. **Every figure states the window it was computed over.** A statistic without
   its span is a number without units. "Projected annual cost: 84.000,00 ₺" is
   not a claim until it says it was taken over eleven weeks — and once it says
   so, the maker can see for himself how much to trust it. Each figure carries
   its own span, because they do not share one: best tank comes from the §5.2
   intervals, km per day from the odometer readings, and the projection from the
   dated records.

2. **The projection is a statistic, not an entry — and §3.3 permits exactly
   that.** §3.3 forbids "estimates, projections *of future entries*… no
   recurrence engine, no reminders, no guessing game", and then says in its own
   parenthesis: "Derived statistics over existing data — averages, projections
   *as statistics* — are fine; creating or suggesting entries the user did not
   make is not."

   §7.3 asks for "projected annual cost" by name. So the rule and the request
   agree, and the line is sharp: TRITIUM may state **what a year would cost at
   the rate the maker has actually spent**, and may not write, suggest, prefill
   or remind anyone of a single future record. F10 computes one number and
   creates nothing.

3. **The projection is spend per observed day × 365, and it refuses to run on
   too little.** Total realised spend ÷ days observed × 365. Below **60 days**
   of observation it reports nothing at all rather than a figure — two fill-ups
   a week apart would otherwise project a five-figure annual cost off a fortnight
   of data, which is not a statistic, it is an accident with a multiplier. The
   threshold is named in one place and shown on the page.

4. **Best and worst tank are §5.2 intervals, and nothing else could be.** The
   lowest and highest l/100km among the intervals the engine already produces —
   which means they inherit every property §5.2 fixed: only between consecutive
   full tanks, partials counted into the later one, no point for the first entry.
   Each is shown with **its date, its distance and its litres**, because "best
   tank: 5,10" invites the question the interval already answers.

   With one interval, best and worst are the same interval, and the page says
   so rather than printing one figure twice under two headings.

5. **Km per day spans the readings, not the calendar.** Lifetime distance ÷ the
   days between the first and last odometer reading. A vehicle bought in April
   and first recorded in August is measured from August: the app knows what it
   was told, and §3.3 keeps it from assuming the rest.

6. **True cost per km is the only place `purchase_price` is spent.** `(lifetime
   spend + purchase price) ÷ lifetime distance`. §7.3 names it, F9 reserved it,
   and it appears **beside the same figure without the purchase price** — the two
   together are the statistic, because one of them falls for the whole life of
   the car and the other does not.

7. **A figure that cannot be computed says so, and says why.** No zeroes standing
   in for unknowns anywhere on this page. "Not enough data yet" with the reason —
   fewer than two full tanks, fewer than two readings, fewer than sixty days —
   is a true statement; `0,000 ₺/km` is a false one (§3.3, and the same
   distinction §5.2 makes between no point and a zero).

8. **The tab is read-only, like the Summary.** No chips either: these are
   lifetime figures, and a statistic filtered to "this month" is a different
   statistic wearing the same label. F7's chips belong to lists and F8's to
   charts; §7.3 asks for a section, and a section is what it gets.

### 2.2 The arithmetic — `src/shared/statistics.ts`

Pure functions returning scaled integers and their spans, tested as arithmetic.
`bestTank`, `worstTank`, `kmPerDay`, `projectedAnnualCost`, `trueCostPerKm`,
`observedDays`.

Day counts are taken between two `YYYY-MM-DD` strings through a single helper
that builds both ends with `Date.UTC` and subtracts — the same UTC-only
arithmetic `range.ts` uses for month ends, for the same reason: it is calendar
arithmetic and must not touch the local offset or a locale (§3.6).

### 2.3 Tests that must exist

- **vitest:** best and worst tank being the extremes of the §5.2 intervals, with
  their spans · one interval making best and worst the same · km per day over a
  known span, and refusing on one reading · **the projection refusing under sixty
  days and computing over sixty-one** · the projection being spend-rate × 365 and
  not a monthly figure × 12 · true cost per km differing from cost per km by
  exactly `purchase_price ÷ distance` · every figure returning null rather than
  zero when it cannot be computed · a day count that does not move when the clock
  crosses midnight in UTC+3.
- **Playwright:** all four §7.3 figures on the page · each stating its window ·
  the purchase price appearing in the true-cost figure and nowhere else · a fresh
  vehicle showing "not enough data yet" for every figure rather than zeroes · no
  control on the page · the tab not scrolling sideways at 1280 × 720 · all of it
  with the network severed.

## 3. SCOPE — OUT

No settings UI (F11) — units stay km, litres and l/100km, and precision comes
from `settings.toml` as it already does. No chips and no filtering on this tab
(decision 8). No new record type, no new file, no new IPC. **No forecast of any
individual future entry, ever** (§3.3) — the annual projection is one aggregate
number and creates nothing. No cost-of-ownership modelling, no depreciation, no
resale estimate: none of those is in §7.3 and every one of them would need a
figure the maker never entered.

## 4. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

F10 is done when every line below is true:

1. `npm test` is green: the five audits, both tsconfigs silent under `strict`,
   the units, the build, and the e2e suite.
2. All four figures §7.3 names are on the page: best and worst tank, km per day,
   projected annual cost, true cost per km including purchase price.
3. **Every figure states the window it was computed over.**
4. The projection reports nothing below sixty observed days, and says why.
5. `purchase_price` appears in the true-cost figure and in no other figure in the
   application.
6. A vehicle with too little data shows a reason on every figure, and no zeroes
   standing in for unknowns.
7. Nothing on the page creates, prefills or suggests a future entry.
8. Every figure is in the family convention — `1.234,56 ₺`, `GG/AA/YYYY` — in
   both languages, with no `Intl` call and no locale read.
9. The tab does not scroll sideways at 1280 × 720.
10. Zero network: the audits still prove it statically and the offline e2e run
    still behaves identically.
11. No AI attribution exists anywhere in the tree or the git history.
