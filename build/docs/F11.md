  surface, and F11 owns settings whole."

- **F10** left every figure on the Statistics page reading `settings.toml` for
  its precision, which until now nothing could change.

So F11 is not a page of switches. It is the milestone where the units become
real, and that reaches into every form, every list, every chart and both
statistics pages. §4.4's schema has held the keys since F2; F11 is what finally
writes to them and what finally reads them.

**The file does not move.** See decision 1 — it is the decision the whole
milestone rests on.

## 2. SCOPE — IN

### 2.1 The decisions this milestone settles

1. **The file stays metric, whatever the settings say.** `fuel.toml` holds
   `odometer_km` and `litres`; `vehicle.toml` holds `tank_capacity_l`. Those key
   names are §4.4's, and they are not decoration — a file whose meaning depended
   on a setting in *another* file would stop being the plaintext, hand-editable
   record §3.4 promises. Open one in Neovim and 19764 is kilometres, always.

   Units are therefore a **boundary**: converted on the way to the screen and on
   the way in from a form, and never in between. `settings.toml` changes what is
   displayed and what a typed figure means; it changes nothing that is stored.

   The consequence is the one that matters: **switching to miles and back leaves
   every file byte-identical.** A test asserts exactly that.

2. **Consumption is converted, never recomputed.** The §5.2 engine keeps
   producing l/100km ×1000, and `km/l` and `mpg` are derived from that one
   figure. Recomputing from litres and distance in another unit would give a
   second arithmetic path to the number F4 built a whole milestone around, and
   the two would drift at the last decimal.

3. **`mpg` means the US gallon, and the app says so.** §4.4 offers `gal` and
   `mpg` without saying which gallon, and there are two — 3,785411784 l and
   4,54609 l — which differ by twenty per cent. Twenty per cent is not a
   rounding difference; it is the difference between a good tank and a bad one.

   TRITIUM uses the **US gallon**, and the settings page prints the litre
   equivalent beside the option rather than leaving the maker to find out from a
   figure that looks wrong. A second setting to choose the other gallon is not
   added: §4.4 offers one `gal`, and inventing a second would be answering a
   question the constitution did not ask.

4. **Every unit is independent, exactly as §8 says.** Distance, volume and
   consumption are three settings, not one system. `mi` with `l` is permitted
   and is not corrected — someone who reads an odometer in miles and buys fuel
   in litres is describing a real car, not a mistake.

5. **Price follows the volume unit, because it has to.** `price_per_litre` is
   stored per litre. With `gal` selected the form asks for and the lists show a
   price **per gallon**, converted at the boundary like everything else. Showing
   a per-litre price beside a gallon volume would produce a total the maker could
   not reconcile with the pump receipt in his hand.

6. **Precision is display-only and always was.** `decimals_consumption` and
   `decimals_cost_per_km` cut a figure down at the moment it is shown; the
   engine keeps three decimals so the setting can be raised later without the
   arithmetic having been thrown away first (F4's own reasoning, now reachable
   from the interface). Range 0–6, which is what `readDecimals` already accepts.

7. **The payment-method list becomes editable, and shipping it is not the same
   as owning it.** §4.4 calls it an "editable list" and names three. F11 adds
   `payment_methods` to `settings.toml`; the three ship as the default, the maker
   may add and remove, and **removing one does not touch a record that uses it**
   — F5 already made the cost form keep an unknown stored value, and that is what
   makes removal safe rather than destructive.

8. **Language and palette move, and nothing about them changes.** F1 built both
   and they work; F11 puts them in the same page as the rest rather than leaving
   Settings looking like two milestones stapled together. Still no detection,
   ever (§3.6).

9. **A settings write is still a merge.** F1's `settings:write` merges over what
   is on disk so that changing a unit cannot erase the currency the maker was
   asked for exactly once. F11 adds fields to that merge and changes nothing
   about how it works.

10. **The currency is shown and cannot be changed.** §4.4 — "asked ONCE at first
    launch, then fixed forever". The page states it, greyed, with the reason
    beside it. Hiding it would leave the maker wondering where it lives; offering
    it would be offering something the constitution forbids.

### 2.2 The boundary — `src/shared/units.ts`

Pure conversions on scaled integers, both directions, tested as arithmetic:

| From the file | To the screen | Back from a form |
|---|---|---|
| `odometer_km` (whole km) | km or miles | miles → km |
| `litres` (×1000) | litres or US gallons | gallons → litres |
| `price_per_litre` (×1000) | per litre or per gallon | per gallon → per litre |
| `tank_capacity_l` (×10) | litres or gallons | gallons → litres |
| l/100km (×1000) | l/100km, km/l or mpg | — (never entered) |
| cost per km (×1000) | per km or per mile | — (never entered) |

Constants named once: **1 mi = 1,609344 km**, **1 US gal = 3,785411784 l**, and
`mpg = 235,214583 ÷ (l/100km)` which follows from the other two.

### 2.5 What measuring it found — miles carry a decimal

The round-trip test written for decision 1 failed, and it was right to.

A mile is 1,609 km, so a **whole mile is coarser than the whole kilometre
underneath it**: 3.907 km and 3.908 km both land on 2.428 miles, and converting
back cannot know which was meant. Measured over 0–300.000 km, whole miles fail
to round-trip for **113.589 of 300.001 values — 37,9%.**

That is not a rounding curiosity. A maker working in miles who opened a fill-up
and pressed Save **without touching anything** had better than one chance in
three of moving his own odometer by a kilometre. The file would have drifted
under him, one edit at a time, which is precisely what decision 1 exists to
prevent.

So **a distance in miles is shown to one decimal**, which makes the display
finer than the storage — 0,1 mi is 0,16 km — and the round trip becomes exact
for every value in that range. `tests/unit/units.test.ts` walks all 300.001 of
them, and a second test pins the whole-mile failure so the decimal is never
tidied away by someone who thinks it is cosmetic.

It is also, incidentally, what a real odometer shows.

Kilometres keep no decimal: the file stores whole kilometres and a decimal there
would invent precision that is not in the data. Volume needed nothing — gallons
are already shown at three decimals, so the display is finer than the litre
underneath and the round trip was exact from the start.

### 2.3 Tests that must exist

- **vitest:** every conversion round-tripping · **km → mi → km returning the same
  integer** for a realistic odometer range · l/100km converting to km/l and mpg
  against figures worked by hand (9,00 l/100km = 11,111 km/l = 26,135 mpg) · the
  US gallon and not the imperial one, asserted by the constant · precision
  cutting a figure without changing the stored one · a removed payment method
  leaving a record that uses it alone.
- **Playwright:** switching distance to miles changing every odometer on screen ·
  entering a fill-up in miles and gallons and **the file still holding km and
  litres** · switching to miles and back leaving `fuel.toml` byte-identical ·
  the price field's label following the volume unit · precision changing what a
  figure shows and not what it is · adding and removing a payment method ·
  the currency shown and not editable · all of it with the network severed.

## 3. SCOPE — OUT

No currency change, ever (§4.4, decision 10). No exchange rates, no conversion
between currencies (§3.1, §8). No imperial gallon (decision 3). No unit
auto-detection from anything — §3.6 forbids reading the OS for language and the
same reasoning covers units. No per-vehicle units: §4.4 puts `[units]` in
`settings.toml`, which is the application's file, not a vehicle's. No theme
editor: the eleven palettes are §8's roster and F4b's values. No import or export
of settings (§4.1 ships neither).

## 4. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

F11 is done when every line below is true:

1. `npm test` is green: the five audits, both tsconfigs silent under `strict`,
   the units, the build, and the e2e suite.
2. Distance, volume, consumption, both precisions, language and palette are all
   settable, and all persist to `settings.toml`.
3. **A fill-up entered in miles and gallons is stored in kilometres and litres**,
   and the file's keys are unchanged.
4. **Switching units and switching back leaves every record file
   byte-identical.**
5. `mpg` is the US gallon, and the interface says which gallon it means.
6. Consumption in km/l and mpg is converted from the §5.2 figure, not recomputed.
7. Changing a precision changes what is shown and nothing that is stored.
8. A payment method can be added and removed, and removing one leaves a cost that
   uses it exactly as it was.
9. The currency is shown and cannot be changed.
10. Every figure is in the family convention — `1.234,56 ₺`, `GG/AA/YYYY` — in
    both languages, with no `Intl` call and no locale read.
11. Zero network: the audits still prove it statically and the offline e2e run
    still behaves identically.
12. No AI attribution exists anywhere in the tree or the git history.
