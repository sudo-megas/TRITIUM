SUMMARY is the first tab in the bar and has been empty since F1. It is the page
the application opens on, so it is the page that decides what TRITIUM appears to
be — and §7.1 already settled what is on it, block by block:

> Vehicle header (name + odometer, no photo) · Gas card (average consumption,
> last consumption, last price + date) · Costs card (this month vs previous
> month) · **Trend cards as a static grid, all visible at once — no carousel** ·
> Last entries · Lifetime totals (spend, distance, litres).

F9 adds no new data and no new file. Every figure on this page already exists in
`fuel.toml`, `costs.toml` and `service.toml`, and every one of them is computed
at read time (§3.7 — derived values are never stored).

**The one thing to get right is the average**, and it is not the obvious thing.
See decision 2.

## 2. SCOPE — IN

### 2.1 The decisions this milestone settles

1. **Everything on the page is realised, and nothing on it is a projection.**
   §3.3 permits "derived statistics over existing data — averages, projections
   *as statistics*" and forbids "creating or suggesting entries the user did not
   make". A card saying what the last three months cost is the first; a card
   saying what next month *will* cost is the second. F9 ships only the first.

2. **The average consumption is the ratio of the sums, never the mean of the
   ratios.** These are different numbers and only one of them is right.

   Take two intervals: 40 l over 400 km (10,00 l/100km) and 10 l over 500 km
   (2,00 l/100km).

   - Mean of the ratios: `(10,00 + 2,00) ÷ 2 = 6,00 l/100km`
   - Ratio of the sums: `50 l ÷ 900 km × 100 = 5,56 l/100km`

   The second is what the car actually did. The first weights a short interval
   exactly as heavily as a long one, and is the number you get by averaging the
   consumption column — which is the obvious implementation and the wrong one.
   `sumLitres ÷ sumDistance × 100`, over every §5.2 interval, on scaled
   integers, rounded once at the end. A unit test holds the two apart with these
   figures.

3. **Lifetime distance is the span between the readings that exist**, highest
   minus lowest, across `fuel.toml` and `service.toml` alike (F6's reasoning).
   It is deliberately **not** the sum of the §5.2 intervals: those cover only
   full-tank to full-tank, so a vehicle whose last fill was partial would
   silently under-report the kilometres it has actually done.

4. **Lifetime spend is every lira from all three files**, income subtracting, and
   it agrees with F8's Monthly Costs summed — the same function computes both, so
   the two cannot drift.

   **The purchase price is not in it.** §4.4 keeps `purchase_price` on the
   vehicle record, and §7.3 gives "true cost per km including purchase price" to
   the Statistics section, which is **F10's**. A lifetime total that quietly
   folded in two million lira would make every other figure on the page unreadable
   and would answer a question this page did not ask.

5. **This month runs to today, and the comparison is honest about it.** The costs
   card compares this month against the previous one using `boundsFor`, which
   ends "this month" at today rather than at the 31st (F7's decision 4). On the
   third of the month that comparison is three days against thirty-one, so the
   card **says which span it is comparing** rather than presenting a fall of 90%.

6. **A trend card compares two realised periods and states both.** §7.1 asks for
   trend cards in a static grid, all visible at once, and forbids a carousel by
   name. Each card carries a figure, the same figure for the previous period, and
   the difference. A period with nothing in it shows no change rather than a fall
   to zero — the same distinction §3.3 makes everywhere else between "zero" and
   "no record".

7. **The last entries block is one list, not three.** Fuel, costs and service
   merged and sorted newest first, each row saying which file it came from. Three
   separate short lists would waste the width and would hide the thing the block
   is for: what has happened to this vehicle lately.

8. **The vehicle header carries the name and the odometer, and no photo.** §4.4
   is explicit — "NO photo field. Vehicles have no photos anywhere in TRITIUM" —
   and §7 repeats it. The odometer is the highest reading known, from either
   file.

9. **An empty summary is the same page holding nothing.** Every card is present
   with its labels, saying it has nothing rather than being replaced (§7 — no
   "get started" screens). A vehicle with one fill-up shows the cards that one
   fill-up supports and leaves the rest empty; it does not hide them.

10. **The summary is read-only.** No buttons, no forms, no entry paths. Adding a
    record is what the FUEL, COSTS and SERVICE tabs are for, and a second way in
    would be a second thing to keep correct.

### 2.2 The arithmetic — `src/shared/summary.ts`

Pure functions over the bundle, returning scaled integers. No formatting, no
React, no colour — tested as arithmetic, in the same way `series.ts` is.

`averageConsumption`, `lifetimeDistance`, `lifetimeSpend`, `lifetimeLitres`,
`latestOdometer`, `lastConsumption`, `lastPrice`, `monthComparison`,
`recentEntries`.

### 2.3 Tests that must exist

- **vitest:** **the average being the ratio of the sums and not the mean of the
  ratios**, with the two intervals above, which differ by 0,44 l/100km · lifetime
  distance spanning both files and not summing §5.2's intervals · lifetime spend
  agreeing with `monthlyCostSeries` summed · income subtracting from it ·
  purchase price absent from it · a month with no previous month showing no
  change rather than a rise from zero · recent entries merged across the three
  files in date order.
- **Playwright:** every §7.1 block present and populated · the odometer in the
  header matching the highest reading in either file · the costs card naming the
  span it compares · **no carousel, no control that hides a trend card** · an
  empty vehicle showing every card, empty · a palette switch leaving the page
  correct · all of it with the network severed.

## 3. SCOPE — OUT

No statistics section (F10) — best and worst tank, km per day, projected annual
cost and true cost per km including the purchase price are all §7.3's, and F9
deliberately leaves the purchase price out so F10 can introduce it where it
belongs. No settings UI (F11). No charts on this page: §7.1 lists cards and a
list, and F8's seven live on their own tab. No entry paths, no editing, no
deletion. No photo, ever (§4.4).

## 4. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

F9 is done when every line below is true:

1. `npm test` is green: the five audits, both tsconfigs silent under `strict`,
   the units, the build, and the e2e suite.
2. Every block §7.1 lists is on the page: vehicle header, gas card, costs card,
   trend grid, last entries, lifetime totals.
3. **The average consumption is `total litres ÷ total distance × 100`**, and a
   test proves it differs from the mean of the per-interval figures.
4. Lifetime distance spans both files; lifetime spend includes fuel, costs and
   service with income subtracting, and **excludes the purchase price**.
5. The costs card states the spans it is comparing.
6. The trend cards are a static grid, all visible at once — no carousel and no
   control that hides one.
7. A vehicle with no records shows every card, empty, with its labels.
8. Every figure is in the family convention — `1.234,56 ₺`, `GG/AA/YYYY` — in
   both languages, with no `Intl` call and no locale read.
9. Nothing on the page projects a future entry or suggests one.
10. No photo appears anywhere, and no field for one exists.
11. Zero network: the audits still prove it statically and the offline e2e run
    still behaves identically.
12. No AI attribution exists anywhere in the tree or the git history.
